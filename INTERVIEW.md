Android App + Spring Boot Interview Prep
Key Project Walkthrough Tips
Explain the problem your app solves and target users. "Instead of directly saying 'I used Spring Boot, React...' I started with why the project exists, what problem it solves, who the users are."

Be ready to discuss architecture and tech choices. "They asked me about the architecture and finally my tech stack."

Walk through one feature end-to-end (UI → API → DB). "Then they asked me to explain my project deeply then when I started to explain my second project he stopped me."

Spring Boot / Backend Prep
Solidify core Spring/Spring Boot concepts and JPA/SQL. "1.prepared stream very well 1-2 stream question will definitely come 2. All major used annotations and their internal working 3.JPA 4.Rest api design"

Prepare common interview topics (security, migrations, integration points). "If I were approaching this, I'd just familiarise myself with the broad concept of what the library is solving... be honest with your boss when they assign you tasks."

Practice simple CRUD + one integration (e.g., auth or payment webhook). "The project uses several technologies... Flyway, JWT-based Spring Security with rate limiting, Spring Batch, Stripe integration, and webhooks."

Android / Mobile Prep
Know lifecycle, coroutines/Flow, Compose, and common architecture patterns. "For mid-level, you mostly need to prove you can implement features independently: lifecycle, ViewModel, coroutines/Flow, networking, Room, DI, testing, Compose/XML."

Be able to explain implementation choices and trade-offs from your app. "Focus on what you actually use at work most first coroutines, flows, threading, architecture, testing basics from your last projects then build a small app from scratch and be ready to walk through it in detail."

Prepare a few concrete metrics or improvements from the app (performance, CI/CD, bugs fixed). "Reduced app startup time by 40% ... Designed a scalable caching layer ... Improved CI/CD pipeline."

Interview Practice and Practical Tips
Expect long waits and simple live coding on the portal; rehearse introductions and project pitch. "They instructed all of us to be online on the interview portal from 9 AM... I was called around 3:30 PM... They first asked me to verify my ID card, then asked me to sit down. Then came the usual... 'Tell me about yourself.'"

Practice explaining one bug or trade-off for each major topic (shows depth). "My rule: for every topic, write down three things: 1. What it is. 2. When you would use it. 3. A real bug/tradeoff related to it."

Don’t fake knowledge; say when unfamiliar and show willingness to learn. "If you have no experience with a particular technology, then say it. If they discover you're faking, you're dismissed at the spot."
